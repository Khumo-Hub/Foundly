01. Scope of these terms

Foundly is a proposed online marketplace intended to connect independent South African businesses with customers. The current website is a private concept preview designed to demonstrate its visual identity and user experience. By visiting it, you are using a demonstration website, not a live transactional marketplace.

02. Sample listings and illustrations

The named stores, product descriptions, prices and images presently shown are illustrative. They are not offers to sell goods, evidence of seller registration or a promise of availability. Clicking “Add to demo bag” only changes the temporary on-screen shopping demonstration; it does not place an order, reserve stock or create a payment obligation.

03. Acceptable use

Please use the preview lawfully and respectfully. Do not attempt to interfere with its operation, access systems without authority, impersonate anyone, introduce malicious code, or reuse its content in a misleading way. Features may change while the site is being designed.

04. Design and content rights

Foundly’s name, website design, copy and original visual assets are presented as brand materials for the project. You should not represent them as your own or suggest an affiliation without appropriate permission. Any seller-supplied content on the future marketplace will need a separate seller content licence and intellectual-property process.

05. Availability and accuracy

We aim to keep this preview usable and informative, but the interface may change or be temporarily unavailable. Demonstration functions should not be relied upon for commercial decisions. Nothing on this page is intended to exclude rights that cannot lawfully be excluded under South African law.

06. Terms required before launch

Before accepting transactions, Foundly will need approved business and contact disclosures, binding buyer and seller terms, a final commission schedule, payment and settlement rules, fulfilment responsibilities, complaints processes, a return and refund policy, and privacy and security disclosures.

Relevant statutory frameworks include South Africa’s Consumer Protection Act and Electronic Communications and Transactions Act. The final terms should be reviewed by qualified South African legal counsel.
